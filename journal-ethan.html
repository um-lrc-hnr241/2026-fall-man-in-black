Entry 1 · September 9

Topic: Unlisted

I used to understand consciousness as a facet of our brain, much like we translate receptor cell signals into bitter or sweet, we translate the holistic set of signals into consciousness. Furthermore I believed consciousness was ‘the powerhouse’ of the brain much like a mitochondria is for a cell; yes it is the most noticeable / seeming base part of the machine, in reality it is just one cog of the cell/cognition.

Freedom is the feeling that you have the ability to choose what you would like to do, think, and believe. While our choices can be influenced by family, experiences, propaganda, etc., etc., at the end of the day we think we are making the choices for ourselves (rather than explicitly thinking we lack choice).

Topic: Unlisted

I think that there is a sense of continuous self, as I feel that I am the same person I was in middle school, just grown up and matured. However, what is to say that this sense of a continuous self isn't just an instantaneous feeling rewritten over past experiences every day. Still, I believe there is a continuum of consciousness in me as I, although I have changed a lot since middle school, some core parts of how I see the world, how I react, and I am entertained by, are all the same. And what is a self if not the aggregation of your thoughts, preferences and perceptions? Although I find the idea of equating Dharma to consciousness quite interesting, I believe that it isn't such an applied literal connection between consciousness and time.

Do we have to know why we are the way we are? Do we choose to define ourselves?

Do we control our memories which shape us? Or are we inherently mysterious to even ourselves?

How has repetitive behavior changed you? Is detachment or discipline more applicable for you?

I find it hard to think of an example of how a repetitive behaviour changed me, as often lose follow through on the repetitive tasks i am so ardently committed to in the beginning, however one example could be learning mandarin, where the constant dedication to one goal has changed how i look at grammar, especially in poetic works, and how i have changed my thinking on what makes a word and what makes a word or phrase special.

I would say discipline is more applicable as I find that outer experience definitely influences my inner ideals and actions. Furthermore, I would agree with how the discipline model relates to the idea of evolution creating human behaviours (such as empathy) for advantages it made through accidental iteration.

Entry 5 · September 30

Topic: Unlisted

Scarry’s phenomenal account of consciousness is that we experience consciousness as a form of projecting our pain onto others, people and objects, and that things like empathy are proof of consciousness as we can thus not only project our pain onto them but project their pain onto ourselves while not feeling their pain but feeling what it would be like to be them and then contrasting that with what it does feel like to be you/me/oneself, she also discusses animism as a step towards this as we imagine our pain being sensed by other objects, taking these non sentient objects and using them to reflect our own sentience as a way to relieve our pain by making them feel some of it and by using them to relieve anger.

Entry 6 · October 7

Wittgenstein on consciousness, freedom, and self

I think that Wittgenstein would describe consciousness as defined by our knowledge of ourselves in relation to how we compete with the others around us and how we use decision making for relative edge in knowing ourselves as like individuals are more important than species ala expressions are more important than definitions.

I think Wittgenstein would prioritize how free an individual feels their relative experience to be over the notion of a defining concept of free. If individuals trump species, than individual experiences should trump discrete definitions. Just because we have the same feeling, doesn't mean we are in the same circumstances, or vice versa, and it depends on which matters more (notion or definition) on how you prioritize freedom.

I think again he would say that the self is the most important part of existence, as it is the most individual of individual traits, however I think he would even say that the self is a category and within it are individual aspects of our selves that only through their relation make up the category of us as an individual. An individual isn't one trait but it is all of the individuals related traits smushed into one being.

Entry 7 · October 8

What does Wittgenstein mean for your notion of consciousness?

I feel that his notion of individuals is interesting, as emphasising how unique every instance is from the next makes me think of two ways in which there are ‘families’ in consciousness. There is the family of consciousness, where a wide variety of people’s different experiences and expressions of consciousness can be related–not necessarily to everyone else’s, but at least one other member of the network–to create a broad encompassing but not limiting definition of consciousness. The other family is the family of our own mind, our own consciousness, which is not defined by a supposed pearl in the center of our minds but rather is the sum of a gazillion different complex brain functions all happening simultaneously in completely different ways that are also not completely different.

Entry 8 · October 13

Gell and consciousness

Gell writes about all decorations on a form serving a purpose and in that sense I am reminded of the thought that our consciousness serves no purpose and is merely decoration for a mundane life of physical states– a viewfinder. Under Gell’s assumptions however, the consciousness then couldn’t do anything as it decorates which inherently applies agency into the object–here our consciousness–and gives it function. In this way, is our consciousness tacky? Is it apotropic? Do we have these complex thoughts to bind us into society of minds, or to repel evil from using our body– these are the questions I imagine Gell would ask if in our course on consciousness.

Entry 9 · November 3

Topic: Unlisted

Hegel’s dialectic to me has made more sense from others discussing and using his tools than he alone explaining his ideas ever did. In part, that could be because of how his original writing was so vague on its topic but those authors who use his ideas put concrete examples into his theoretical frameworks. A silly example I like of the dialectic comes from the lyrics of Cardi B’s Up, where in the opening two lines create a–as supposed by some tiktok lyricists–Hegelian dialectic, each alone bearing a small meaning but together forming a relationship where each individual line tells a broad story when united and where each individual line on its own is strengthened by its role in the story. This, and the concrete examples from class helped me see how the ideas of Hegel could be put into play on consciousness, how two unrelated banal potentials can, through interaction, comparison, and competition, create in each other true consciousness and between each other a fluctuating association on–not a solution to–their different issues. I.e Master and Slave (Hegel’s broad example), Colonizer colonized, Bourgeoise and proletariat, ego and Id, etc. What I have not yet understood from Hegel entirely is his relationship with phenomenality, something he supposedly invented, as I only see hints of this concept when looking at how a master and slave can relate to each other.
